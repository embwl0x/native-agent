# Synthesis — A4

## 1. Summary

The packet documents two intertwined Chrome extension regimes: the wind-down of Manifest V2 and the
loosening of declarativeNetRequest (DNR) limits that was offered as compensation. On MV2, the
enterprise exemption granted by the `ExtensionManifestV2Availability` policy was originally described
as running "until June 2025" (f67dbbf9, 2023-11); the later source (095a5599, 2025-03-31) both
confirms that date as a floor ("until at least June 2025") and fixes the terminal event — MV2 support
is removed in the Chrome 139 branch, making Chrome 138 the last version that runs MV2 extensions, and
then only when paired with the policy key. On DNR, the limits moved in stages and the packet's own
sources overtake each other: the static ruleset counts of 05158f86 (Chrome 120: 50 enabled of 100
total, up from 10 of 50) and the dynamic-rule split of 2741637b (Chrome 121: 30,000 for "safe" rules,
5,000 for everything else) are restated and refined by the current API reference a65606cd (2025-06).
Two restrictions survive all of this unchanged: blocking webRequest listeners remain available only
to policy-installed extensions under MV3 (688c66f7), and the elevated 30,000 dynamic-rule ceiling
applies only inside the named scope of "safe" actions — block, allow, allowAllRequests, upgradeScheme.
The review-skipping benefit (e8621005) is likewise scoped: it is minutes-fast only for safe changes
confined to static rule files.

## 2. Findings

1. **The enterprise MV2 exemption is time-bounded, and the binding date comes from the newer source.**
   Rests on f67dbbf9 (2023-11) and 095a5599 (2025-03-31) in combination. f67dbbf9 promised policy-using
   enterprises "one additional year - until June 2025" and said such browsers "will not be impacted by
   the rollout of the deprecation until that time"; 095a5599 restates this as "until at least June
   2025" and then names the removal mechanism: the Chrome 139 branch begins in June, removing MV2
   support. Anyone reading f67dbbf9 alone would treat June 2025 as a migration deadline with an open
   question about what happens after; only 095a5599 supplies the answer, so an enterprise should plan
   against the Chrome 139 branch, not against a calendar date.

2. **Chrome 138 is the last MV2-capable version, and only with the policy key.** Rests on 095a5599.
   The text is explicit: "Chrome 138 is the final version of Chrome to support Manifest V2 extensions
   (when paired with the ExtensionManifestV2Availability key)." A fleet that needs MV2 to keep working
   must pin at or below 138 and keep the policy set; pinning alone is not stated to be sufficient.

3. **The Chrome 139 cutover is not gradual.** Rests on 095a5599. Unlike the earlier disabling waves,
   which "gradually rolled out to users," this "change will impact all users on Chrome 139 at once."
   A rollout plan that assumes a staged ramp — canarying a subset of the fleet onto 139 to observe
   breakage — is wrong; breakage is total on arrival.

4. **Default-off is not the same as removed, and the intermediate state still permits user re-enabling.**
   Rests on 095a5599. As of March 2025 all users on all channels have MV2 extensions "disabled by
   default, but users continue to be able to turn their Manifest V2 extensions back on." Support advice
   given in that window should point users at re-enabling; the same advice becomes false at 139.

5. **The static ruleset limits changed at Chrome 120, and the pre-120 numbers are now historical.**
   Rests on 05158f86. Previously 50 lists shippable with 10 enabled simultaneously; from Chrome 120,
   up to 50 enabled simultaneously and 100 rulesets in total. A filtering extension that architected
   around a 10-list active budget can restructure to 50, and anyone quoting "10 enabled" as the current
   ceiling is quoting a superseded figure.

6. **The 30,000 dynamic-rule ceiling is scoped to a named, closed list of actions.** Rests on 2741637b
   and a65606cd in combination. 2741637b defines "safe DNR rules" as "rules with an action of block,
   allow, allowAllRequests or upgradeScheme" and says the higher limit applies to those from Chrome 121;
   a65606cd confirms the constants — `MAX_NUMBER_OF_UNSAFE_DYNAMIC_RULES` at 5,000 and
   `MAX_NUMBER_OF_DYNAMIC_RULES` at 30,000 from Chrome 121. Any action outside that four-item list is
   budgeted against 5,000, so a developer planning rule capacity must first classify rules by action,
   not count them in aggregate.

7. **Unsafe rules consume the safe budget too; the two limits are nested, not additive.** Rests on
   a65606cd. "Any unsafe rules added within the limit of 5000 will also count towards this limit." An
   extension cannot plan on 30,000 safe plus 5,000 unsafe; the practical safe headroom is 30,000 minus
   whatever unsafe rules are in place.

8. **Session rules are a separate 5,000 budget, and the pre-Chrome-120 pooling is gone.** Rests on
   a65606cd. Up to 5,000 session rules (`MAX_NUMBER_OF_SESSION_RULES`); "Before Chrome 120, there was
   a limit of 5000 combined dynamic and session rules." An extension that previously had to ration a
   shared pool can now budget the two independently on Chrome 120+, and any capacity calculation
   carried over from the pooled era understates available room.

9. **Blocking webRequest survived into MV3 but only inside the policy-installed scope.** Rests on
   688c66f7. `webRequestBlocking` is "Required to register blocking event handlers. As of Manifest V3,
   this is only available to policy installed extensions," and a blocking handler requires both
   `webRequest` and `webRequestBlocking` in the manifest. A store-distributed MV3 extension must be
   built on declarativeNetRequest; an enterprise deploying by policy retains the blocking path.

10. **Review skipping is a scoped exception, not a general fast lane.** Rests on e8621005, with its
    "safe" terminology anchored by 2741637b. Chrome approves an update "in minutes" only "If the only
    changes are for safe modifications to an extension's static rule list for declarativeNetRequest."
    A developer who wants fast filter-list updates should ship rule-only updates separately from code
    changes, since bundling any other change forfeits the expedited path.

11. **The packet's own static-rule totals are stated at two different granularities and should not be
    conflated.** Rests on 05158f86 and e8621005. The first counts *rulesets* (50 enabled, 100 total);
    the second counts *rules* ("bundle up to 330,000 static rules and dynamically add a further
    30,000"). These are compatible but measure different things, so a capacity claim must say which
    unit it uses; neither source states a per-ruleset rule cap.

12. **Where currency matters: a65606cd (2025-06) is the most recent DNR source and should govern.**
    Rests on a65606cd against 2741637b and 05158f86 (both 2023-11). The 2023 blog posts describe
    changes as forthcoming ("This is shipping in Chrome 120", "Starting with Chrome 121"); the API
    reference states them as current and adds the nesting rule and the session-rule split. An answer
    drawn only from the 2023 blog posts would miss that unsafe rules count against the safe ceiling.

## 3. What you could not determine

- **Regex rules.** The packet never mentions regex or `regexFilter` rules, their count limits, their
  memory/complexity constraints, or whether they are classed as safe or unsafe. Nothing here settles
  what a regex-heavy ruleset may contain.
- **Remotely hosted code.** No source in the packet addresses MV3's treatment of code fetched at
  runtime, remote scripts, or what execution of externally hosted logic (if any) is permitted.
- **Whether `redirect` and `modifyHeaders` are the only unsafe actions.** 2741637b defines the safe set
  affirmatively but never enumerates the unsafe complement, so the packet establishes only that
  anything outside the four named actions is capped at 5,000.
- **What "safe modifications" means for review skipping.** e8621005 uses the phrase without defining
  it. It is plausible that it tracks the safe-rule definition in 2741637b, but the packet does not say
  so, and it does not state whether adding a new static ruleset file (versus editing an existing one)
  qualifies.
- **The exact Chrome 139 stable date.** 095a5599 says the branch "will begin" in June 2025 and that
  removal lands with 139; it does not give a stable-release date, so the real-world cutover day is
  not fixed by the packet.
- **Post-139 enterprise options.** No source says whether any policy, enterprise channel, or extended
  support path exists for MV2 after 139; 095a5599 says only that support is removed.
- **Firefox and Safari specifics.** 05158f86 notes both "provided early input" and support raising the
  limits, but the packet gives no numbers or timelines for those browsers.
- **Version rollback.** e8621005 mentions "version roll back launched last month" without describing
  its mechanics or constraints.

ceiling, not the 30,000 safe one, and ~12,000 is over twice that; a65606cd adds that any unsafe rules
