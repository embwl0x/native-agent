# Document T09
## Summary

The packet covers two distinct regimes: the Manifest V2 deprecation timeline and the
declarativeNetRequest / webRequest rule limits. On the timeline, the packet contains three
snapshots of a moving deadline (2023-11, 2025-03-31, 2025-07-24), and only the latest two are
current: the 2023-11 promise of an enterprise exemption "until June 2025" has been consumed by
events, and the operative facts now are that Chrome 138 is the last version in which MV2
extensions function under the `ExtensionManifestV2Availability` policy, and that the policy
itself is removed in Chrome 139. On rule limits, the packet establishes that the ceilings were
raised twice (Chrome 120 for static rulesets and for the combined dynamic+session pool, Chrome
121 for dynamic rules), but that the raised dynamic ceiling is scoped: the 30,000 figure applies
only to rules whose action is `block`, `allow`, `allowAllRequests` or `upgradeScheme`, and the
old 5,000 limit survives intact for everything else. Blocking `webRequest` survives into MV3 only
inside a named scope — policy-installed extensions. The packet says nothing at all about static
rule counts, regex rules, remotely hosted code, or review timelines.

## Findings

1. **[79250f1d, 095a5599 — combination]** Both sources agree that Chrome 138 is the last version
   in which MV2 extensions can run, and that the enterprise carve-out ends with Chrome 139:
   095a5599 states Chrome 138 is "the final version of Chrome to support Manifest V2 extensions
   (when paired with the ExtensionManifestV2Availability key)", and 79250f1d states the policy
   "will be removed with Chrome 139" and that MV2 extensions "will cease to function for any user
   upgrading to Chrome 139". *Consequence:* an enterprise planning migration should treat the 139
   branch, not a gradual rollout, as the hard cutoff and pin to 138 only as a short bridge.

2. **[79250f1d vs. 095a5599 — combination]** The two snapshots differ on the user-level state at
   Chrome 138. 095a5599 (2025-03-31) says users "continue to be able to turn their Manifest V2
   extensions back on"; 79250f1d (2025-07-24) says that with Chrome 138 "users can no longer turn
   them back on." The later source supersedes the earlier on this point. *Consequence:* do not
   advise a non-enterprise user that a toggle exists to re-enable MV2 on 138.

3. **[f67dbbf9 — superseded]** f67dbbf9 (2023-11) grants enterprises using the policy "one
   additional year — until June 2025" and says policy-enabled browsers "will not be impacted by
   the rollout of the deprecation until that time." This is the stale source in the packet: its
   end-date is restated by 095a5599 (exempt "until at least June 2025") and then replaced by the
   concrete version-based cutoff in 79250f1d. *Consequence:* any answer that cites only f67dbbf9
   would give a date rather than the operative version boundary, and would miss that the policy
   key is deleted outright at 139.

4. **[2741637b, a65606cd — combination]** The Chrome 121 increase to 30,000 dynamic rules is
   scoped by action type, not general. 2741637b defines "safe" DNR rules as those with an action
   of `block`, `allow`, `allowAllRequests` or `upgradeScheme`, and states "the rule limit for all
   other dynamic net request rules stays at 5,000"; a65606cd confirms the split via the two
   constants, `MAX_NUMBER_OF_UNSAFE_DYNAMIC_RULES` (at least 5,000) and
   `MAX_NUMBER_OF_DYNAMIC_RULES` (30,000), and adds that unsafe rules added within the 5,000 limit
   also count toward the 30,000. *Consequence:* a developer budgeting rules must count rules by
   action, not in aggregate, and must not assume a redirect- or modifyHeaders-heavy ruleset gets
   the 30,000 headroom.

5. **[a65606cd]** Session rules have their own ceiling of 5,000
   (`MAX_NUMBER_OF_SESSION_RULES`), and the *combined* dynamic-plus-session 5,000 limit existed
   only before Chrome 120 — a65606cd states this twice. *Consequence:* on Chrome 120+ a developer
   can budget session and dynamic rules independently instead of dividing one 5,000 pool.

6. **[05158f86]** Static rulesets: before the Chrome 120 change an extension could ship 50
   rulesets with 10 enabled simultaneously; the change raises this to 100 shipped with 50 enabled
   simultaneously, and the blog notes 50 is "significantly higher than the limit of 20 requested
   in the WECG." *Consequence:* a filtering extension targeting Chrome 120+ can offer users far
   more concurrently active lists, but should keep a fallback for anything older.

7. **[688c66f7]** Blocking `webRequest` was not removed in MV3, it was narrowed to a named scope:
   `webRequestBlocking` "is only available to policy installed extensions" as of MV3, and a
   blocking handler requires both `"webRequest"` and `"webRequestBlocking"` in the manifest.
   *Consequence:* an enterprise-distributed extension can still use blocking interception, while a
   Web-Store-distributed one must migrate to declarativeNetRequest.

8. **[05158f86, 2741637b, a65606cd — combination]** The two 2023-11 blog posts describe the
   limit increases as forthcoming ("This is shipping in Chrome 120", "Starting with Chrome 121"),
   and the 2025-06 API reference confirms the dynamic-rule numbers as shipped and names the
   constants. *Consequence:* the dynamic-rule figures can be relied on as current; only the
   static-ruleset figures in 05158f86 lack a confirming reference-doc source in this packet.

## What you could not determine

- Whether the static-ruleset increase in 05158f86 actually shipped as described, and what the
  per-ruleset or total *static rule* counts are — the packet contains no static rule limits, only
  ruleset counts, and no post-2023 reference doc confirms them.
- Anything about regex rules: no limit, no constant, no restriction on regex filtering appears
  anywhere in the packet.
- Anything about remotely hosted code, the MV3 code-execution restrictions, or `userScripts` —
  the packet does not raise the topic.
- Anything about Chrome Web Store review timelines, expedited review, or how review treats
  rule-file-only updates.
- The exact behaviour of MV2 extensions on Chrome 138 *without* the policy set beyond
  "disabled, cannot be re-enabled" (79250f1d) — whether disabled means uninstalled or merely
  inert is not stated.
- Whether Firefox and Safari adopted the same numeric limits; 05158f86 says only that they
  "provided early input" and support increasing the limits.
