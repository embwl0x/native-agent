## Summary

The packet documents two intertwined Chrome extension policy tracks: the Manifest V2 phase-out and the loosening of declarativeNetRequest (DNR) limits in Manifest V3. On MV2, the enterprise `ExtensionManifestV2Availability` policy was a genuine but time-boxed exemption — first granted an extra year to June 2025 (f67dbbf9), then confirmed and terminated at Chrome 139 (095a5599), with Chrome 138 named as the last version where the policy still works. On DNR, the limits moved upward repeatedly and in ways that superseded earlier figures: the 10-of-50 enabled-ruleset limit became 50-of-100 in Chrome 120 (05158f86), the combined 5,000 dynamic+session cap was split before Chrome 120 (a65606cd), and Chrome 121 introduced a two-tier dynamic rule ceiling where 30,000 applies only to "safe" actions while everything else stays at 5,000 (2741637b, a65606cd). One restriction survived every change: blocking `webRequest` listeners remain available in MV3 only to policy-installed extensions (688c66f7). A scope-limited concession is review skipping, which applies only to safe modifications of static DNR rule lists (e8621005). Several older figures in the packet — the 10-of-50 rulesets, the 5,000 combined dynamic+session limit, and the June 2025 enterprise horizon — are no longer current and would produce wrong answers if relied on.

## Findings

1. **Sources: f67dbbf9 + 095a5599 (combination).** The enterprise policy exemption was extended to June 2025 and then ended, not renewed: f67dbbf9 (2023-11) promises policy-using enterprises "one additional year – until June 2025," and 095a5599 (2025-03-31) confirms exemption "until at least June 2025" and states that MV2 support is removed in the Chrome 139 branch, hitting all users at once. *Someone relying only on f67dbbf9 would plan migration around a policy that still protects them; the pair shows the protection expires with 139.*
2. **Source: 095a5599.** Chrome 138 is explicitly the final version supporting MV2 extensions, and only when paired with the `ExtensionManifestV2Availability` key. *A fleet would pin at 138 and treat any move to 139 as the hard cutover, not a gradual rollout.*
3. **Source: 095a5599.** For non-policy users, MV2 extensions are already disabled by default on all channels but users can still re-enable them manually. *Support guidance would tell ordinary users their MV2 extension is off-by-default and re-enablable, rather than gone.*
4. **Sources: 05158f86 + a65606cd (combination).** The pre-Chrome-120 ruleset regime was 50 shipped lists with 10 enabled simultaneously; Chrome 120 raised enabled-simultaneously to 50 and total rulesets to 100 (05158f86). 05158f86 is the only source giving the old numbers, and it presents them as already superseded. *A developer would stop designing list menus around a 10-enabled cap.*
5. **Sources: 2741637b + a65606cd (combination).** The 30,000 dynamic-rule ceiling from Chrome 121 is scope-limited to "safe" rules, defined as actions `block`, `allow`, `allowAllRequests` and `upgradeScheme` (2741637b); all other dynamic rules stay at 5,000, exposed as `MAX_NUMBER_OF_UNSAFE_DYNAMIC_RULES`, and unsafe rules added within that 5,000 also count toward the 30,000 (a65606cd). *A developer would count rules by action type rather than assuming a flat 30,000 budget.*
6. **Source: a65606cd.** Session rules have their own limit of 5,000 (`MAX_NUMBER_OF_SESSION_RULES`); before Chrome 120 dynamic and session rules shared one combined 5,000 limit. *Code written against the old combined cap would under-use the now-separate dynamic budget.*
7. **Source: 688c66f7 (2025-02, the most recent webRequest source here).** `webRequestBlocking` is still required for blocking event handlers and, as of MV3, is available only to policy-installed extensions; the handler needs both `webRequest` and `webRequestBlocking`. *A store-distributed MV3 blocker must use declarativeNetRequest instead of blocking webRequest.*
8. **Source: e8621005.** Review skipping is a named-scope exception: only when the *only* changes are safe modifications to an extension's static DNR rule list does Chrome approve the update in minutes. *A developer would split rule-only updates into their own release to keep the fast path.*
9. **Sources: e8621005 + 2741637b/a65606cd (combination).** e8621005 (2024-05) describes the combined headline budget as 330,000 static rules plus a further 30,000 added dynamically; the API reference and the Chrome 121 blog qualify that 30,000 as the *safe*-rule figure. *Read alone, e8621005 would overstate the dynamic budget for a redirect-heavy or otherwise unsafe ruleset.*

## What you could not determine

- Regex rule limits: the packet never mentions regex rules, their count ceiling, or any constraint specific to them.
- Remotely hosted code: nothing in the packet addresses whether MV3 extensions may fetch and execute code at runtime.
- Whether the 330,000 static-rule figure in e8621005 (2024-05) is still current, and how it decomposes across the 100 rulesets from 05158f86 — no source states a per-ruleset static limit.
- What happens operationally to policy-managed MV2 extensions after Chrome 139 (removal, silent disable, uninstall) — 095a5599 says support is removed but not the mechanics.
- The definition of "safe modifications" for review-skipping purposes (e8621005) and whether it matches the safe-action list in 2741637b — the packet does not say.
- Whether any source postdates 2025-06 (a65606cd) on these limits; the packet gives no later revision.
