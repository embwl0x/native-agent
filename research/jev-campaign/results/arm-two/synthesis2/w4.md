## Summary

The packet covers two distinct regimes: the end-of-life of Manifest V2 in Chrome, and the numeric limits governing declarativeNetRequest (DNR) filtering under Manifest V3. On MV2, the packet contains two versions of the same document (the "Manifest V2 support timeline") dated 2025-03-31 and 2025-07-24; the later one supersedes the earlier on the state of Chrome 138 and on the fate of the enterprise policy, so an answer drawn from the March excerpt alone would be out of date in one respect and still correct in another. On DNR, the packet shows a set of limits that were *raised* (static rulesets enabled simultaneously, and the dynamic-rule ceiling for a named class of "safe" rules) alongside limits that survived those changes unchanged (unsafe dynamic rules at 5,000, regex rules at 1,000 per type, 2KB per compiled rule). The recurring structure is that each relaxation is scoped — to a Chrome version, to a rule-action whitelist, or to policy-installed extensions — and applying the relaxed number outside its named scope gives the wrong answer.

## Findings

1. **Chrome 138 is the last version on which MV2 extensions can run, and only via the enterprise policy.** Rests on 095a5599 and 79250f1d together. 095a5599 states Chrome 138 is "the final version of Chrome to support Manifest V2 extensions (when paired with the ExtensionManifestV2Availability key)"; 79250f1d states that at Chrome 138 ordinary users can no longer re-enable MV2 at all. Consequence: a fleet that needs MV2 must pin at 138 *and* hold the policy, not one or the other.

2. **The enterprise exemption is removed, not merely expired, at Chrome 139.** Rests on 79250f1d, which supersedes 095a5599. The March excerpt frames the exemption as lasting "until at least June 2025" with removal of MV2 *support*; the July excerpt states the ExtensionManifestV2Availability policy itself "will be removed with Chrome 139." Consequence: there is no policy setting to keep MV2 alive past 138 — planning must be migration, not configuration.

3. **The MV2 rollout changes gradually but the 139 cutoff does not.** Rests on 095a5599 and 79250f1d, which agree: the 139 change "will impact all users on Chrome 139 at once" / "affect all users on Chrome 139 simultaneously." Consequence: staged-rollout mitigation strategies that worked for the earlier disabling waves do not apply here.

4. **Answering from the March 2025 excerpt alone produces a stale answer about user re-enablement.** Rests on 095a5599 versus 79250f1d. 095a5599 says "users continue to be able to turn their Manifest V2 extensions back on"; 79250f1d says that at 138 "users can no longer turn them back on." Consequence: support guidance telling users to flip MV2 back on is wrong from 138 onward.

5. **The 30,000 dynamic-rule ceiling is scoped to an enumerated set of rule actions.** Rests on 2741637b and a65606cd together. 2741637b defines "safe" rules as those with an action of block, allow, allowAllRequests or upgradeScheme, and says the 30,000 limit applies to those starting Chrome 121; a65606cd repeats this and adds that unsafe rules added within the 5,000 allowance also count toward the 30,000. Consequence: sizing a ruleset by the 30,000 figure is only valid if the rules use those four actions.

6. **The unsafe dynamic rule limit survived the Chrome 121 increase unchanged at 5,000.** Rests on 2741637b and a65606cd. 2741637b: "The rule limit for all other dynamic net request rules stays at 5,000." a65606cd names it MAX_NUMBER_OF_UNSAFE_DYNAMIC_RULES. Consequence: any action outside the safe four (redirect, modifyHeaders, etc. — the packet names only the safe set, so the rest is "all other") is capped at 5,000 regardless of Chrome version.

7. **Session rules are a separate 5,000 bucket, and were only separated at Chrome 120.** Rests on a65606cd, which gives MAX_NUMBER_OF_SESSION_RULES = 5,000 and states twice that before Chrome 120 there was a single 5,000 limit *combined* across dynamic and session rules. Consequence: on pre-120 Chrome an extension cannot plan 5,000 of each; on 120+ it can.

8. **The static ruleset limits were raised from 10-of-50 to 50-of-100 in Chrome 120, and that raised state is still current.** Rests on 05158f86 and 7c82d4f5 together. 05158f86 (2023-11) announces the increase shipping in Chrome 120; 7c82d4f5 (2025-06, API reference) confirms the present numbers as up to 100 declared in "rule_resources" with 50 enabled at a time (MAX_NUMBER_OF_ENABLED_STATIC_RULESETS). Consequence: the announcement can be relied on as still describing current behaviour, which is not true of the MV2 announcement excerpts.

9. **Static rulesets carry a guaranteed floor, not a fixed cap.** Rests on 7c82d4f5: enabled rulesets "are guaranteed at least 30,000 rules," named GUARANTEED_MINIMUM_STATIC_RULES. Consequence: 30,000 is the number you may design against, but the wording does not promise it is the maximum available.

10. **Regex rules are capped at 1,000 per type, independent of every other limit above.** Rests on 7c82d4f5: "the total number of regular expression rules of each type cannot exceed 1000" (MAX_NUMBER_OF_REGEX_RULES), plus a 2KB compiled size per rule. Consequence: a regex-heavy design must be re-expressed as non-regex patterns; the raised static and dynamic ceilings do not help it.

11. **Blocking webRequest survives in MV3 only inside a named installation scope.** Rests on 688c66f7: webRequestBlocking is "only available to policy installed extensions" as of MV3, and blocking handlers require both "webRequest" and "webRequestBlocking". Consequence: an extension distributed through the store cannot use it; an enterprise force-installed one can.

12. **Chrome's raised static-ruleset limits exceed what the cross-browser group asked for, and were coordinated with other vendors.** Rests on 05158f86, which notes the 50 enabled figure is "significantly higher than the limit of 20 requested in the WECG" and that raising the limits "is supported by both Firefox and Safari." Consequence: a developer targeting multiple browsers should not assume all three implement the same number, only that all three support raising it.

## What you could not determine

- Whether remotely hosted code is permitted under MV3, and what alternatives exist. The packet contains no source touching this.
- Extension review timelines, or what makes a review fast or slow. No source in the packet discusses review at all.
- The complete list of DNR rule actions. The packet names the four "safe" actions and refers to everything else as "all other" rules; it never enumerates the unsafe ones, so "redirect is unsafe" follows only by exclusion from the safe list.
- Whether the 30,000 GUARANTEED_MINIMUM_STATIC_RULES figure is ever exceeded in practice, and by how much.
- The exact reconciliation between 2741637b and a65606cd on the 5,000 constant's name: 2741637b calls the 30,000 figure MAX_NUMBER_OF_DYNAMIC_RULES while a65606cd assigns MAX_NUMBER_OF_UNSAFE_DYNAMIC_RULES to the 5,000 figure. The numbers agree; the packet does not fully clarify the naming overlap.
- Whether anything between the March and July 2025 MV2 excerpts changed the June 2025 milestone itself — the July excerpt drops the "until at least June 2025" language without explaining it.
- What happens to a Chrome 138 enterprise fleet that does *not* upgrade: the packet says nothing about extended support, LTS channels, or how long 138 remains serviced.

## Frozen question set

**Q1.** Yes — on Chrome 138 with ExtensionManifestV2Availability set, MV2 extensions still run; 138 is explicitly the final version to support them when paired with that policy key. Once the fleet moves to Chrome 139 the policy is removed and MV2 extensions cease to function, for all users on 139 simultaneously rather than by gradual rollout. Sources: 095a5599, 79250f1d.

**Q2.** No. Redirect is not among the four "safe" actions (block, allow, allowAllRequests, upgradeScheme) that qualify for the 30,000-rule ceiling, so such rules fall under the unchanged 5,000 limit — MAX_NUMBER_OF_UNSAFE_DYNAMIC_RULES — and 12,000 is not achievable; note also that unsafe rules within that 5,000 count against the 30,000 figure. Sources: 2741637b, a65606cd.

**Q3.** No. The total number of regular expression rules of each type cannot exceed 1,000 (MAX_NUMBER_OF_REGEX_RULES), and each rule must additionally be under 2KB once compiled; the larger static limits (100 rulesets, 50 enabled, 30,000 guaranteed minimum rules) do not lift the regex cap. Source: 7c82d4f5.

**Q4.** Yes, but only if it is a policy-installed extension — as of Manifest V3 the "webRequestBlocking" permission is available only to those — and the manifest must declare both "webRequest" and "webRequestBlocking". Source: 688c66f7.
