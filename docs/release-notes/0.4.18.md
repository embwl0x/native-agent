# NativeAgent 0.4.18

A new look and a simpler way in. The agent gets a desktop of its own that it
reads like a page, does a whole job on your Mac or in your browser in one go,
and asks for what it needs with a card right in the chat. You choose how much
it keeps in mind, and the meter tells you in real tokens.

## A new look, and a Simple view

- Soft coloured light drifts behind the window. Pick its colour in Settings
  or from the Simple view's settings menu.
- **Simple view**: the agent, the other agents it works with and your helpers
  on one panel beside the chat. Talk to any agent or helper directly; a
  working one shows a moving rim. Switch to Advanced any time.
- The gear at the foot of the Simple view opens a small menu: colour, the
  warmth switch (dark mode) and the way to every other setting.
- Every page (Today, Desk, Memories, Trust, Connectors, Providers, Dreams and
  the rest) is rebuilt in the same calmer style, with plainer words.
- **Agent view** shows what the agent sees on its own desktop, read-only.
- **A friendlier first run**: a new install opens in the Simple view. The agent
  asks what you want it to be, then how it should sound — energetic, calm,
  playful, blunt, or like someone you name — and keeps both. Its first hello
  offers to set things up just by talking, with a card right in the chat.

## The agent's own desktop

- Everything the agent can reach opens by name as a page it reads: mail,
  messages, notes, calendar, reminders, music, files, GitHub, Slack, Notion,
  its helpers, the people and agents it talks to, and the app's own settings.
- Home is one laid-out page: what changed since it last looked, what is
  waiting on you, today's calendar and reminders, and its open windows.
- A name opens; it never acts. Anything that changes something shows its
  choices first, so a click on the wrong row can't do the wrong thing.
- Windows it saved (a mail inbox, a browser tab) come back as they were after
  a restart.

## One call for a whole job

- On your Mac the agent does a short run of steps in one go (open, type,
  choose, press, save) and checks each step landed before the next.
- Menu commands and keyboard shortcuts press the real menu item and are
  checked by what they did.
- It never takes over the app you are using: if you switch apps mid-step,
  it stops rather than put its app back over yours.
- In your browser, reading a page costs far less, scrolling shows only what
  is new, a page and a form can be read and sent in one step, and pages keep
  loading in a background tab while you are away.
- Music plays a song or playlist by name, reminders complete by title, the
  calendar takes a plain day, market quotes take a watchlist, and mail opens
  a message straight from the list instead of searching the whole inbox.
- X is read through your browser, not a paid X account, in a background
  tab rather than over what you are looking at.
- A web search from the agent's desktop reads the results privately instead
  of opening a Google tab in your Chrome, and a tab it closes leaves its
  desktop.

## Tools that answer plainly

- The agent's inputs are taken the way it naturally writes them, and a
  failed call says what to do next.
- Finding a tool is one step, and a tool brings the rest of its family with
  it when there is room.
- A missed file names what is in the folder; GitHub links work as pasted.
- An empty calendar or reminder list says access is fine; services that are
  not connected say nothing was searched.
- Gmail reads HTML-only mail, long Notion pages say they were cut short, and
  a revoked Notion connection asks to reconnect.

## Cards in the chat

- Anything you can connect has a card in the chat: providers, connectors,
  Chrome, iPhone and macOS permissions. A card settles by itself once the
  permission or key is really in place, and an ask that is already done
  shows as done.
- **GitHub sign-in**: connect with your GitHub account in the browser. A
  personal token still works if you prefer, and a network blip no longer
  ends the sign-in.
- A fresh install with no provider points at the Providers card beside the
  message.

## Context window

- New setting in Settings: **Model default** or a **Custom** size. The agent
  keeps in mind 60% of the model's window, or your custom size if that is
  smaller, and every part of the app sizes to the same number.
- The context meter counts real tokens and names the model whose window it
  measures. In a bot's conversation it measures the bot's model, and it
  updates as soon as you change the setting.

## More natural speech

- The agent no longer picks up repeated nicknames or pet phrases from its own
  past replies. When it does lean on one, it is told plainly so it can stop.

## Steadier

- Long replies that start repeating themselves are stopped early and keep
  what came before; stopped turns say they stopped.
- Stop stops the turn you meant, even one that started a moment after.
- The first greeting tries again when a turn ends with no reply, and says so
  honestly.
- Replies to Telegram send reliably even when one part is slow.
- Connected agents: busy slots free up for new conversations, retries are not
  refused as different messages, and a large attachment no longer fails the
  whole reply.
- Grok Bot: a follow-up goes out after 2 minutes instead of 10, and a late
  answer still lands in the chat that asked.
- A desktop agent's conversation opens and carries on after a send it
  could not confirm, and what you typed in an agent's thread shows as yours.
- The list of people and agents no longer reads the Keychain row by row.
- Mail actions can no longer hold everything else up on a very large inbox.
- Lower idle CPU across the Simple view and the window's light.
