# NativeAgent 0.6.21

Rewrite your agent's words and it learns from your version, phone messages
arrive sooner, and the app speaks in plain sentences.

## Your version of its words

- On the Mac, Revise… on any of your agent's messages opens its words for you
  to change. Your version is sent as your message, and both versions are kept
- Your agent reads both versions on its next turn. Where it is unsure what
  your change meant, it offers you the other version, and your next choice
  settles it
- A corrected fact is taken as a correction, not as a matter of taste

## Your agent's abilities and repairs

- When your agent does by hand what one of its saved abilities already does,
  it is pointed to that ability, or to the step where its last run failed
- When a part of the app your agent felt failing works again, your agent is
  told once; staying busy is not taken as a fix
- Home and Doctor show why a repair is owed, dated by its latest evidence

## iPhone

- While your agent is replying, a draft shows Send beside Stop, so your
  message reaches it in the turn it is in
- Messages between the phone and the Mac arrive sooner: each side announces
  what it sent
- A Mac whose clock runs fast no longer skips messages from the phone
- Fixed chats sometimes not reaching the iPhone
- Location is a new phone sense, off until you turn it on. The phone sends
  its last known position as it moves, and your agent looks it up when needed
- Notification replies go to the Mac they came from, Lock Screen Mute stays
  muted, Answer again replaces the answer you chose, and Load earlier always
  loads more
- A message a quit or crash cut short no longer leaves the phone waiting

## Plain words

- Approval cards say who asked, what and why. Decline is offered wherever you
  answer an approval: the Inbox, a notification, the phone
- An approved action that has not finished reads Running, never Unknown
- Errors, pairing and health read as plain sentences on the Mac and the phone
- The phone and the Mac show the same short pairing code
- A sign-in you cancel says cancelled, and first launch shows one setup state
- A Telegram voice note that cannot be read gets one plain reply saying what
  happened and what to send

## Search and the web

- Web search gives your agent every usable result, taken in turn across the
  search engines, instead of the first engine's
- Direct answers and info boxes from search engines are kept
- A search is retried once when its connection had already closed

## Mac control

- Scroll and drag no longer hang, and each is confirmed by what moved: the
  scroll bar or the text selection
- Under Full Mac your agent sees the screen as you do, unmasked; text it saves
  is still redacted
- Prose in apps like TextEdit is no longer hidden as a possible secret
- Commands your agent runs find programs the way your Terminal does
- Continuing a long page no longer silently starts again from the top
- Cancelling dictation at the microphone prompt stops it before the
  microphone starts

## Memory and the night

- The nightly dream is no longer refused by the daytime limit on reflections
- Memories in Spotlight no longer disappear after a month
- Forgetting a memory removes every copy of it

## The Desk

- A pen circle takes only the cards inside its ink
- Cards on the table show their Desk number and the same state as the list

## Steadier

- A turn no longer hangs for minutes when your agent reads one of its past
  receipts
- Archiving a chat while your agent is replying stops the reply first, so the
  chat does not come back
- One interrupted write no longer blocks restoring every archived chat
- After a relaunch, unsent messages go out in the order you sent them, and a
  message the Mac accepted is never dropped because its queue was full
- A sent message no longer returns as a draft after switching chats
- A habit set for a fixed time never runs twice
- Telegram and Slack deliver only where they are still allowed to
- Signing out of xAI or Codex clears the whole sign-in

## Privacy

- Chat titles, previews and reply notifications are redacted before they are
  shortened, so a key cut in half never shows

Requires an Apple silicon Mac running macOS 26 or newer.
