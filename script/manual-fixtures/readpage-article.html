Reading the whole page

A careful reader keeps the complete paragraph together. The text before an inline element, the emphasized words, and the sentence after it belong to one thought. The supporting evidence stays beside the claim it supports, so the next reader can follow it directly.

A second paragraph can contain several different inline elements without becoming a list of fragments. Punctuation stays attached to the words that precede it, including an exact reference. A URL used as its own label appears once: https://example.org/source.

  • The first item has one line and a source.
  • The next item stays on its own line.

A final paragraph proves the read reached the end. Hidden content, scripts and page chrome do not belong to the article; the visible paragraphs do. This paragraph contains enough prose to distinguish a complete article from a script shell with only a heading and a consent prompt. Read the results, or the relative report.
